Side note, why am I doing this after I wrote up my EDA?

You’re right to be confused! If you were doing this analysis for real, this code would have appeared somewhere in step 7, before you wrote up your EDA. Its here to try and balance the workload across the lab but I know it will feel slightly out of sync in terms of the data analysis process

We now want to formally calculate the difference between 2010 and 2018. Calculating differences is however, slightly more complex than it might first appear. Expand each of these to read the rules for different data types (you will need this info in step 9A):

Firstly, always start with the later time-stamp (e.g. 2018 - 2010). You want increases to be positive and decreases negative

Use ‘percentage points’ when comparing percentages. Expand to read more


If you are comparing two percentages e.g. how does the percentage-tree-cover change over time, then you need to use percentage points.

  • A percentage point is the the simple arithmetic difference between two percentage values.
    Value1(%) - Value2(%). E.g. if your index of 20% increases by two percentage points, it means it goes from 20% to 22%.
  • The percentage change is the actual percentage your value fell by. E.g. if your index of 20% increased by 2%, then you’re saying it has gone from 20% to (20*1.02)%. AKA 20% to 20.4%. AKA it has increased by 2% of 20%.
  • This is one way people “lie with statistics”


Use raw differences or percentage change when comparing numeric variables. Expand to read more


Some variables, like population, are numeric (e.g. the population in 2010 was 1249 and in 2018, the population was 1502). In this case, we can either

  • Calculate at the raw difference (1502 - 1249 = 253 more people). This is simple to understand, but over a whole map, areas where the population was high to start with might swamp any view of what’s happening
  • OR.. we calculate the percentage change. (1502 - 1249)/1249 = 0.203. So the population increased by 20%.


For monetary variables, its useful to also take inflation into account. Expand to read more


Inflation tends to mean that things get more expensive over time. If we don’t take this into account, all you see on a map of differences is that life “became more expensive”, which would likely swamp any spatial pattern.

A simple method to achieve this is to convert the 2010 values into 2018 dollars using an inflation adjustment factor (more details here - https://www.bls.gov/cpi/).

What do I mean by this?

  • Take the 2010 Consumer Price Index (the cost of a standardised “basket” of goods and services e.g. a bottle of milk, a gallon of fuel), and divide it by the 2018 CPI (the cost of the same “basket” of goods and services in 2018)

  • So the Inflation adjustment factor for 2010-2018 is `2010CPI / 2018CPI`

  • Example: If you bought a car in 2010 and wanted to know how much that purchase would have cost in 2018, you can simply calculate:
    `2018CarCost = 2010CarCost * (2010CPI / 2018CPI)`

  • This means the raw inflation adjusted difference in car price between 2010 and 2018 is:

    `RawChange = 2018CarCost / (2010CarCost * (2010CPI / 2018CPI))`

  • And the percentage inflation adjusted difference in car price is:

    `PercentageChange = (2018CarCost / (2010CarCost * (2010CPI / 2018CPI)) - 1) * 100`


Where can you I get the numbers for the CPI?

  • Click here - https://www.bls.gov/cpi/research-series/r-cpi-u-rs-allitems.pdf. This will take you to a pdf document with the US Department of Labor’s CPI for every month and year from 1977-2019.

  • As an example of reading the table, the average CPI for 1979 is 114.3.

inflation_adjustment <- cpi_2010/cpi_2018


ChicagoTracts_Change <- ChicagoTracts_2018 |>
   left_join(st_drop_geometry(ChicagoTracts_2010) |> select(-arealand_sqmiles),
             by = "GEOID") |>
   mutate(
      #--- Percentages: 2018 minus 2010, in percentage points
      # canopy is a percentage, its just not in the variable name
      chg_canopy      = canopy_2018       - canopy_2010,
      chg_poverty     = pct_poverty_2018  - pct_poverty_2010,
      chg_vacant      = pct_vacant_2018   - pct_vacant_2010,
      chg_white       = pct_white_2018    - pct_white_2010,
      chg_black       = pct_black_2018    - pct_black_2010,
      chg_hispanic    = pct_hispanic_2018 - pct_hispanic_2010,
      chg_degree      = pct_degree_2018   - pct_degree_2010,
      
      #--- Counts: percent change
      chg_population  = 100 * (population_2018 - population_2010) / population_2010,
      
      #--- Dollars: percent change after adjusting for inflation
      chg_income      = 100 * (med_income_2018 / (med_income_2010 * inflation_adjustment) - 1),
      chg_rent        = 100 * (med_rent_2018   / (med_rent_2010   * inflation_adjustment) - 1),
      chg_value       = 100 * (med_value_2018  / (med_value_2010  * inflation_adjustment) - 1)
   ) |>
   #--- Keep just the ID, land area and the change columns
   select(GEOID, arealand_sqmiles, starts_with("chg_"))
